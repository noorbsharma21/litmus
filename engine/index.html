The material

Forty of my drawings, paintings and photographs. They are what the structures compose with, so the hand on a generated page is literally mine. They are no longer read for taste: the engine tried that, wrote thirteen claims off them, and was wrong about twelve.

Every piece has been read and tagged. Click any one to open its record.

The things that are not pictures

A corpus of images only teaches the engine how things look. Most of what I actually go back to is a song or a poem, and what carries over from those is structure: how long before it gets going, how much is left out, whether it repeats, how it ends. Those are layout questions in a different costume, so they are asked that way.

These are not my work, so they sit apart from the corpus and are labelled that way in the file. They bias what the engine reaches for. They do not become rules.

Which of these two is better, and why

Every other judgment in this engine was a refusal, and refusals only clear the floor. Nothing here had ever asked which of two good things is better, so there was no signal in it about the top end at all.

Each pair answers the same question two opposite ways. A preference between two good answers to one question transfers to a page neither of us has seen. A preference between two unrelated pages does not. Open both before you pick.

A pick with no reason is recorded at zero weight. The reason is the whole payload.

What the galleries agree on

This stage used to subtract a list of things any competent designer does from the tags the engine read off each piece. The tags were the engine's inference, and inference from pictures came back wrong twelve times in thirteen, so that half is gone. The counts below stay, because they were counted rather than read.

What the galleries agree on

Subtracting a consensus means knowing what it is, so I counted one instead of assuming it. These are siteinspire’s own style filters with the number of sites under each, read on 30 October 2026. Minimal is tagged on 847 sites. Pattern is tagged on 19. That ratio is the thing worth noticing: the corpus above is mostly pattern.

Telling it where it is wrong

The kept tags get written as statements specific enough to argue with. I mark each one. The false ones matter most, because a first read of anyone’s taste is a description of good design until they correct it. Marking here rewrites the file in the next step.

Rules in your own words

The claims above were written by the engine out of the corpus. These are written by you, carried into every prompt word for word, and never paraphrased. Say what is good or what is bad, and scope it if it only applies to one kind of thing.

What comes out

One page, built from whatever survived the last step. Refusals first, because a model will do anything you have not forbidden. Change a mark in step four and this changes.


    

      
      
    


    
The same taste, four different jobs

    
Four structures built from the file above: a matrix, a board, a map and a plate.
      They should not look like each other, because they are not the same object. All four are still
      sheets, and the one finding settled by comparison says a page should answer the person rather
      than sit there, so all four are known to be short of the standard. If one looks wrong in a way
      none of your rules would catch, mark it: that is the gap worth finding.

    

    

      
      
    

    

  


  
  
  

    
What it decides, and why

    
Type what the thing is. The engine asks seven questions of it, in the order
      that decides what kind of object it is, and the answers pick a structure. The reasoning is
      kept and shown, because a choice you cannot see the reasoning for is not a choice you can
      correct.

    
Until now a brief picked one of five skeletons and all five were a stack of
      bands down a page. Nineteen of the forty-four references are a map, a plan, a network or a
      projection, and none of those five could make any of them. These seven are read off the
      references instead, and each one names the pieces it came from.

    
Where nothing fits, the engine says nothing fits. Falling back to a stack of
      bands is the failure this stage exists to end.


    

      
      
    

    


    

    

  


  
  

    
Putting it to work

    
Start a brief is the full version: the narrative, what is real, images, the shape, the feel, references and the output, before anything is written.

    
Say what you are making. The engine wraps it in the file and hands back the
      whole instruction, ready to paste anywhere.

    
The prompt no longer sets type size, weight, measure or leading. Those used to be
      drawn from a field built out of my guesses and labelled as a choice. The only value each run
      sets is the ground, drawn from the measured counts. Above everything sit the rules that are
      not matters of taste at all: contrast, minimum sizes, line length.

    
The brief is read first and it outranks the file on anything to do with
      function. The engine also picks only two or three signature moves per run out of everything
      it knows, because using the whole vocabulary at once is the failure, not the goal. Halftone
      belongs in the file. It does not belong on every page.
