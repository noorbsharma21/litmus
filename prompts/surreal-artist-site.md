Build A personal website for a surreal artist. Forty paintings and collages, plus the notes and source scraps behind them.

What follows is one person's taste, written down. It is not a template and
not a checklist. The hard parts are marked hard and the rest is direction.
Where this file and the brief disagree about what the thing has to do, the
brief wins. Where they disagree about how it should look, this file wins.
Serve the brief first, then make it look like this.

THE STRUCTURE  this outranks everything about surface and scroll
  board   Everything on one sheet, placed by argument, dense, with a data
          strip along the foot.
  why        Five references put unlike things on one sheet and let the
             density be the content.
  read off   P03, S06, S19, P23
  Build that, not a hero followed by sections. One sheet, forty-plus pieces
  on it, placed so that two things sitting next to each other are sitting
  next to each other for a reason. Hairline connectors between the ones that
  belong together. The notes and scraps go on the same sheet as the
  paintings, not on a separate page about process.

  Not a landing page. Across forty-four reference pieces there is not one
  hero, not one call to action, not one row of sections down a scroll. A
  brief that sounds like it wants that form is asking for the one form this
  taste contains no example of.

RULES THAT APPLY HERE  17 of 19, by scope
  Never: Never write: authentic, hidden gem, off the beaten path, vibrant,
    nestled, cool, cute, vibe, must-visit, Instagrammable, bucket list,
    trending, a find, a score.
  Never: No em dashes in any prose. Rephrase the sentence instead.
  Never: No knowing asides like "that is the whole trick". Use a concrete
    detail instead. An aphoristic closing line is fine.
  Never: Never use the negation flip: "X is not A, it is B". Say what it is.
  Never: Never fill type with a gradient.
  Never: Never use translucent blurred panels.
  Never: Never open with a rounded pill badge announcing a feature, with or
    without a sparkle.
  Never: Never pair a filled primary button with a ghost secondary beside it.
  Never: Never use a row of three rounded cards with soft drop shadows.
  Never: Never show a row of large round statistics with captions under them.
  Never: Never run a row of greyed-out logos under the hero.
  Never: Never use a centred testimonial with a circular avatar under it.
  Never: Never close on a dark band with a glowing CTA.
  Never: Never write supercharge, seamless, effortlessly, unlock, empower,
    or introducing.
  Never: Do not use hairlines, dividers or rules to create structure. Space,
    alignment, weight and position separate things. A rule is allowed only
    where it marks something space cannot: the head of a data table, a real
    edge of the page, or a line that carries information such as a
    connector, a route or a scale bar.
  Always: When a rule comes out, give the space back. A block that was
    separated by a line needs roughly double the gap it had, or it reads as
    one block.
  Always: Where a line was labelling a change of section, use a small label
    in the quiet colour instead, not a line and not nothing.

MOVES FOR THIS RUN  take these 3, leave the rest of the vocabulary alone
  When you need a backdrop, reach for an image before you reach for a fill.
  Put several images together. One picture on its own is not a composition.
  Choose the ground before the subject and give it a treatment of its own.

THE MEASURED PALETTE  sampled off the references, not chosen
  Grounds. Thirty-three of forty-four sit at 88% lightness or higher, and
  they split two ways with almost nothing between: nineteen are dead
  neutral, white or a grey with zero cast, and thirteen are a warm paper
  between hue 35 and 51.
    #FFFFFF  white
    #F3EBE0  paper, hue 35, the commonest warm ground in the set
    #ECE5D1  card
    #E4DEC9  kraft, the darkest paper before the darks begin
    #FEFBEA  cream, graph paper
    #FDFF70  the colour ground, hue 61, which took 46% of one page

  Ink. Twenty-four pieces carry a dark, twelve of them warm, two cool,
  mean lightness 12. The dark end is a warm near-black.
    #1A1614  ink
    #110B0A  deep
    #382F2C  soft, for text below full strength

  Accents, with how many of the forty-four carry each family: red 7,
  yellow 6, orange 5, blue 3, magenta 2, cyan 2, green 1, violet 1. Reach
  for them in roughly that proportion.

    #C5242B  vermilion
    #F4432D  bright red
    #EE5884  pink
    #F64FB2  magenta, riso, sits straight on the acid yellow
    #E47937  orange
    #FCF02A  acid yellow
    #FDFF70  highlighter
    #EDD164  soft yellow, the lowest hue any yellow here reaches
    #072493  ultramarine
    #3866C1  cobalt

    #0F6E2B  bottle green, one piece in forty-four

  Absent, and this is the strongest part, because no amount of looking at
  the work would tell you to avoid these. Each was looked for and is not
  there.
    A cool off-white ground. One in forty-four. The default page colour of
      a generated marketing site, #F8F9FA and its neighbours, is the thing
      to refuse.
    Mid-saturation, mid-lightness colour. Nothing lives between 20% and
      45% saturation at 45% to 60% lightness. Colour is either deep or
      bright. The muddy middle is absent.
    A golden or amber yellow. Nothing below hue 48. An amber yellow is the
      wrong yellow.
    Teal, mauve, pastel. One violet in one riso cover, no teal at all. The
      cool side of this palette is cobalt and nothing else.
    A dark page ground. Zero of forty-four.
    A saturated mid green. Greens are a dark bottle green or a pencil olive
      at low saturation.

HOW A PHOTOGRAPH GETS TREATED  never a wash
  Flat colour over it as though overprinted. P07 silhouettes the cattle in
  oxide red over a photograph of a field. S03 knocks the figures out of a
  street photograph in highlighter yellow. P21 screens the whole thing to
  halftone. S19 runs the photographs as a blue and red duotone. If there is
  texture it comes from a printing process, never a filter laid on top.

WHAT THE REFERENCES ACTUALLY CARRY  counted, not inferred
  Forty-four pieces. Pieces organised as a map, plan, diagram or network:
  nineteen. Pieces carrying numbers, coordinates, codes, legends or a scale
  bar: sixteen. Pieces where something is drawn or written by hand:
  fourteen. Pieces either very dense or very sparse with little in between:
  twenty-five. Dark grounds: zero. Rounded corners, soft shadows, cards:
  zero. Hero sections with a call to action: zero.

  So: number the works. Put a legend or a key somewhere. Let the annotation
  be a graphic element rather than administration. Something on the sheet
  should be made by hand and look it, and a handwriting typeface is not
  that. Pick an extreme on density. A sheet two thirds full is the failure
  state, because it has neither the argument of a full one nor the nerve of
  an empty one.

NON NEGOTIABLE  not matters of taste, and they outrank everything above
  Body text at 4.5:1 contrast against whatever sits behind it. Display text
    at 3:1.
  Check contrast against the actual ground, not against white. A coloured
    field changes it.
  Never let colour alone carry meaning. It must also be in position, weight
    or wording.
  Body text never below 15px, and never below 16px on a phone.
  Line length between 45 and 80 characters. Narrower is better than wider.
  Anything you tap is at least 44 by 44 points.
  Text over an image needs a scrim, a solid panel, or a crop that guarantees
    the contrast.
  Every image carries alt text. Every control reachable by keyboard.
  A dense board has to survive a phone. Decide now whether it reflows into a
    single indexed column or pans as a sheet, and say which in the code.
